A ⅛″ plywood top drops into the rim and glues onto a shelf that runs round the inside. The neck screws into an integral, ribbed neck block. Optional access panel in the back.

Printing and assembly
  • Print the body back face down, open side up, as one part. No supports: the walls and the neck block go straight up, and the underside of the glue shelf is a 45° staircase (0.4 mm steps). With screw wells on, the flat round roof of each well is a short bridge.
  • Material: PETG (or ASA) — it creeps less than PLA under string and screw load. 0.2 mm layers.
  • 7 walls / perimeters (0.4 mm nozzle): the 3 mm rim and the shelf print fully solid. 6 top and bottom layers, so the 3 mm back floor is solid too.
  • Neck block: a ribbed shell (pocket walls, gussets, ties, a plate under the pocket and screw bosses), so normal infill (15–25%) is fine — the 7 perimeters make its walls solid. The cells under the pocket are sealed and close at 45°, no supports.
  • Top: cut the ⅛″ ply from the top panel SVG/DXF (1:1, millimetres; check the 100 mm bar on a paper print first). It drops into the rim and sits on the shelf; the notch fits round the neck pocket. Glue it to the shelf and the top of the neck block (wood glue or epoxy), weighted flat.
  • Neck: drill pilot holes in the neck through the body's holes, then screw on from the back (through the back plate, or with washers / ferrules in the wells). Snug, not cranked.
  • Access panel (if on): print the cover plate face down (the STL is the right way up). It drops into the bevelled recess flush with the back and is held by small screws into the ring round the opening.

All sizes in millimetres in the files. One body STL, plus the cover plate when the access panel is on. See README.md in the repo for the design notes.
